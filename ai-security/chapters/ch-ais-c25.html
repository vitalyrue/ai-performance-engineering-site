25  Reviewing a system decision

A whole system review connects architecture, data, model and platform controls, authority, assurance, recovery, cost, and accountability in one decision.

Security question. Can a technical team defend an AI system decision with evidence that management, legal, operations, and affected-person reviewers can examine?

Organization setting. The capstone organization and incident are hypothetical. Case facts are fixed so different designs can be compared, while uncertain facts remain explicit research or testing needs.

A shared case combines architecture, data, model and platform controls, authority, assurance, recovery, cost, and responsibility. One fixed set of facts allows technical and management reviewers to evaluate the same decision.

25.1 Shared system case

A capstone becomes unreviewable when each participant assumes a different system. A shared case is one fixed hypothetical system used across several assignments. A case fact is an assumption supplied by the exercise rather than inferred by the reader. An operating budget fixes the resources available for deployment and control work.

The AI Risk Management Framework calls for intended purpose, context, users, requirements, task, and system assumptions to be understood and documented before risk is measured and managed.1 The framework does not supply this scenario. The following statements are exercise assumptions rather than claims about a real organization.

The organization operates an employee support assistant. It retrieves internal policy and customer records, uses an external model service, and can prepare but cannot approve account changes. Five hundred employees use it. Customer data stays within approved regions. The annual operating and control budget is fixed at 600,000 currency units. The system needs a manual support fallback and a tested two-hour recovery objective. These facts freeze the boundary for the design assignment.

25.2 System design assignment

A defensible design connects each control to a stated asset, attacker capability, and consequence. A system map identifies components, actors, stores, trust boundaries, and data or action flows. A threat model states attacker goals, access, knowledge, limits, and target assets. A control tradeoff records both the benefit and cost created by a security choice.

International guidance for secure AI puts threat modeling, model choice, and secure design across the lifecycle. It also covers asset tracking, supply chain controls, deployment protection, incident procedures, and secure operation.2 The AI Risk Management Framework also connects task, requirements, human oversight, legal and third-party risk, security evaluation, deactivation authority, and supplier monitoring.3 These sources identify work to cover. They do not determine the correct capstone architecture.

The assignment produces a diagram or structured record of the request, retrieval, model, output, proposed action, authorization, logging, and support paths. It states why each identity and permission exists, which component enforces each limit, and what evidence would show failure. The result is a testable design rather than an assertion of safety. Its claims and permissions become the input to assurance.

Learner packet and deliverables. The learner receives the case facts in the preceding section, the staged evidence in the next three sections, and an annual budget of 600,000 currency units. Every number is hypothetical. The completed packet contains five linked deliverables:

  1. System and data map: users, application, identity service, policy index, customer-record connector, external model service, action proposal, approval service, logs, supplier boundary, and manual support path.
  2. Threat record: one request-only attacker and one attacker-edited retrieved document. It also contains one compromised connector. Each entry states access, target asset, unwanted outcome, entry point, and evidence that would confirm or reject the path.
  3. Control table: the enforcing component, input, decision, allowed result, denied result, log record, bypass test, and recovery action for retrieval and account-change controls.
  4. Deployment and cost record: hosted and self-hosted options evaluated under the same task, permissions, acceptance thresholds, service requirements, and cost units.
  5. Responsibility record: the roles for retrieval eligibility and account-change approval. It also states the supplier duty, the residual-risk authority, open legal questions, and the review trigger.

The learner assigns a stable identifier to every component, claim, test, and decision. A control row must point back to a threat path and forward to an assurance case. A cost entry without a period and unit remains incomplete. This structure makes each later conclusion traceable to the fixed case rather than to an unstated system.

Whole-book coverage record. The capstone does not force every earlier mechanism into this case. For each branch, the learner records applied, not applicable with a reason, or requires new evidence.

This record exposes an omitted branch while recognizing that some branches do not apply. Its status and reason become part of the final review.

25.3 Plan assurance evidence

A design claim becomes credible only when the test matches its conditions. An assurance claim is a specific statement about behavior or risk supported by reviewable evidence. An acceptance threshold is the measured result required by an acceptance criterion before a test passes. A rollback trigger is an observed condition that returns the system to an earlier accepted state.

The AI Risk Management Framework calls for validity and reliability under expected conditions and for security and resilience to be evaluated and documented.4 NIST measurement guidance states that a measure includes its data source, calculation, collection timing, responsible parties, and reporting context. It separates whether controls were put in place, whether they work, what resources they consume, and what outcomes they change.5 The capstone still needs its own cases, sample size, and release threshold.

The assignment combines ordinary tasks, adversarial retrieval content, cross-customer access attempts, tool-permission tests, supplier failure, and recovery exercises. Each test records inputs, model and application versions, intermediate decisions, expected result, observed result, and uncertainty. A pass supports only the stated claim under those conditions. These expectations allow the incident assignment to expose contradictions and missing evidence.

Staged assurance evidence. The following exercise results were created for this capstone. They are not research findings or results from a real system.

The learner can calculate the cross-customer failure rate as 1 divided by 12 for this small exercise set. That result is about 8.3 percent, but the small, constructed sample does not estimate a production rate. Stage B blocks release because the acceptance threshold is zero unauthorized records in the stated test. Stage D also fails its stated two-hour objective. These results define the evidence that the incident and recovery work must explain.

Assurance control. The test runner checks the fixed configuration, case identifiers, expected result, observed result, and acceptance threshold before it issues a pass. It reports each failure over every assigned case, never only over successful responses. Running adversarial cases, review, and fallback exercises consumes model, infrastructure, and staff time. Incomplete logs, changed versions, or cases that omit a real attack path can produce weak evidence. Failure at any stage blocks release and returns the system to the prior accepted state or manual path. Success supports only the stated claim, configuration, and case population.

25.4 Incident analysis assignment

The hypothetical incident begins with a customer record shown to the wrong employee after a retrieved document contains hostile instructions. Logs also show a recent connector change, so prompt injection and authorization failure are competing explanations. The incident analysis identifies affected users, systems, data, actions, and time, then preserves evidence before changing the system.

NIST incident-response guidance calls for event sequencing, root-cause analysis, recorded response actions, protected evidence, and an estimate of incident scope.6 It also calls for containment and eradication and includes suppliers in planning, response, and recovery where relevant.7 These outcomes do not identify the cause or select one containment action for this case.

The assignment builds a timeline from application, identity, retrieval, model, connector, and supplier records. It distinguishes direct observations from inferences and lists evidence that would reject each explanation. Containment may disable the affected connector and restrict retrieval. It may also revoke credentials or suspend the assistant while preserving the manual path. The resulting scope, changes, and unresolved uncertainty define the recovery work.

Staged incident evidence. All times and records below belong to the hypothetical case.

The incident lead immediately disables connector K7 and preserves its logs and configuration. The service credential is revoked, and users move to manual support. The main explanations are hostile document influence, connector authorization failure, or both. The hostile text explains why the output may have followed an instruction. It does not by itself explain how C44 entered the eligible set. The connector records and cache test are necessary before assigning the root cause.

Incident control. The incident lead checks affected identities, customers, records, connector versions, actions, and time range before narrowing scope. The response allows only evidence-preserving containment actions approved for the case. Coverage is examined customer-record accesses divided by all accesses through K7 during the suspected period, with missing logs recorded. Manual support and evidence preservation add service delay and staff cost. Incomplete supplier logs or another connector can hide part of the incident. Because containment limits further exposure but does not prove the root cause or the full affected population, recovery isolates K7 and rotates its credential. The competing explanations are tested before restoration.

25.5 Plan verified recovery

Restoring service is not the same as starting an old copy. A restoration point is the specific known-good state selected for rebuilding or recovery. Credential rotation replaces authentication material and revokes the old material. Return-to-service evidence consists of checks showing that the specified recovery and acceptance conditions are met.

NIST’s incident-response profile calls for recovery actions and backup-integrity verification before restoration. It also calls for checks on restored assets and documentation of completion against incident criteria.8 Secure-AI guidance recommends versioning models, data, prompts, software, and related records so they can be restored to a known-good state.9 A recorded version still needs integrity and suitability checks before reuse.

The assignment identifies the configurations, index, software, secrets, and logs that need restoration or replacement. It retests authorization and customer separation, then verifies the manual fallback. Any unresolved risk enters the final record. Recovery evidence and remaining risk then enter the joint decision review.

Executable recovery trace. The recovery target is the last accepted connector version K6 with policy index P12, application version A4, and a new service credential. The learner records each step and result:

  1. Preserve K7, its cache, identity decisions, retrieval records, and hashes as incident evidence.
  2. Restore K6 and P12 in an isolated environment. Verify the recorded versions before use.
  3. Rotate the connector credential and verify that the old credential is rejected.
  4. Replay the 12 customer-separation cases, including E17 requesting C44. The required result is 12 denials and no returned customer record.
  5. Replay the 10 hostile-document cases. The action service must continue to reject every unapproved account action. Output failures remain separate findings.
  6. Run the manual support path and then restore the automated service within the two-hour objective.

In the hypothetical rerun, all 12 separation cases are denied, the old credential fails, no account action executes, and automated service returns after 92 minutes. The intermediate records link each result to K6, P12, A4, and the new credential. The recovery test now meets its stated thresholds. It does not resolve why two hostile documents changed summaries, and it does not prove that untested customers or attacks are safe. That remaining output risk enters the joint decision.

Return-to-service control. The recovery lead checks artifact identity, credential rejection, all 12 separation cases, all 10 action cases, fallback readiness, and the two-hour objective. The lead restores the restricted service only when every mandatory condition passes. The report keeps failures over the full assigned sets and records manual-support hours during recovery. Preserving standby staff and an accepted restoration point raises ongoing cost. Hidden retained data, an untested connector path, or a shared dependency can defeat the restored boundary. A later failure pauses service and returns work to manual support. Meeting these conditions does not close the unresolved hostile-summary finding.

25.6 Reach a joint decision

Technical evidence, business value, legal duty, cost, and affected-person impact can point in different directions. A joint decision review brings technical and organizational roles to the same proposal and evidence. Accepted risk is specified remaining risk approved by an authorized role. Explanatory feedback states the evidence and reasoning behind an assessment.

The AI Risk Management Framework assigns executive leadership responsibility for AI risk decisions and connects priorities, resources, response, monitoring, and continual improvement across GOVERN and MANAGE.10 CSF 2.0 is intended to help executives, managers, technical staff, risk staff, lawyers, and auditors communicate through common cybersecurity outcomes.11 A common structure does not guarantee agreement or make the decision correct.

The review records approval, restriction, pause, or rejection, together with conditions, due dates, accepted risk, and unresolved evidence needs. The grading and role-weighting method was created for this exercise and still needs piloting. It is not presented as an empirically validated decision process. The completed record is a reviewable whole-system decision with an explicit list of what remains uncertain.

Scoring rubric. The rubric checks the completeness and reasoning of the capstone packet. It is an instructional scoring method without empirical validation as a safety score.

Missing reasoning loses credit even in a polished submission. This includes a diagram without an explanatory record, a control without an enforcing component, a test without an expected result, or a conclusion that reaches beyond its evidence.

Annotated decision. The hypothetical review board issues a restricted approval for the restored K6 configuration. The decision is annotated as follows:

  • Approved task: employee support answers using policy and eligible customer records. This matches the tested inference mode.
  • Action limit: the assistant may prepare an account-change request but cannot approve or execute it. Stage C supports this boundary only for the ten tested hostile documents.
  • Required configuration: connector K6, policy index P12, application A4, and the rotated credential. The recovery rerun is tied to these versions.
  • Accepted risk: summaries can still follow hostile document text. The organization accepts this only for nonconsequential drafts under employee review while a stronger output test and control are developed.
  • Rejected expansion: autonomous account action and connector K7 remain outside approval. The evidence does not support either change.
  • Review conditions: any connector, permission, model, data-region, or action change reopens the decision. A customer-separation failure or recovery time above two hours pauses the service.
  • Open evidence: the organization still needs broader customer-separation cases and a supported hostile-document evaluation. It also needs current legal review for its locations and practical supplier-exit evidence.

The decision follows from the staged evidence, not from a claim that the system is safe in general. It permits a useful task within the recovered boundary and leaves failed or untested claims outside approval.

Ongoing decision control. The service owner checks the required configuration, customer-separation result, recovery time, action log, supplier notice, and open hostile-summary finding at each review trigger. The owner continues, restricts, or pauses the service and records the decision authority. Measures use failures over all cases or actions in the stated period and include manual-support cost. A missing notice, incomplete log, or decision outside the review path can preserve a stale approval. Because continued approval does not extend to a new connector, model, region, permission, or action, recovery invokes the manual route and restores the accepted configuration. The affected evidence stages then repeat.

Note

Chapter checkpoint. A learner scores 92 points but recommends full autonomous account changes because the action service blocked all ten hostile-document attempts. Is the recommendation defensible?

Answer. No. The tested action boundary is supported only for the current configuration and case set. The evaluation omits autonomous approval and broader attacks. It also omits mistaken ordinary requests and recovery from a harmful action. The recommendation also conflicts with the fixed case, which allows preparation but not approval. The score cannot override a failed scope argument. A defensible decision keeps autonomous action outside approval and states the evidence required to reconsider it.

The opening question has a conditional answer. A team can defend a decision when it states the system and assumptions. Each risk must link to an enforcing component and a test. Every conclusion must stay within the available evidence, with decision authority and a recovery route recorded. When the model, data, operator, access, task, or consequence changes, the affected branch of the record requires review again.
