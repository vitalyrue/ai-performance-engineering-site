21  Assign responsibility

A responsibility map assigns decisions and evidence across developers, providers, deployers, data owners, purchasers, and affected people.

Security question. Who is responsible for each AI system decision, and which legal, contractual, and governance duties apply?

Organization setting. A hypothetical organization buys model access and builds an application around it. The application connects internal data and serves employees or customers. Several parties can affect the same outcome, but their authority and duties differ.

The inventory shows which parties operate components and make decisions. Responsibility follows those acts and authorities, while legal and contractual duties depend on use, location, role, and date.

21.1 Roles and responsibilities

Responsibility becomes unclear when a project calls every outside party a vendor and every internal party an owner. The analysis begins with the act each party performs. A provider legally or contractually supplies an AI system or model. A deployer uses an AI system under its authority. An affected person is a person whose rights, safety, access, or interests may be affected.

The European Union AI Act defines a provider as the party that develops or has an AI system developed and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own identity. It defines a deployer as a party using an AI system under its authority.1 For covered high-risk systems, branding, substantial modification, or a change in intended purpose can cause another party to assume provider obligations.2 These definitions apply only within the Act’s scope and dates. A project still needs a system-specific responsibility map.

The map links each lifecycle decision to authority, evidence, and escalation. A supplier may operate inference. The organization selects the data and grants connector access. It also decides if an output affects a customer. An affected person may have no operating role but still requires consideration in risk decisions. The role map identifies which obligations need examination next.

Responsibility trace. In the hypothetical employee assistant, the supplier operates model inference, the application team selects retrieved fields, the identity service checks the employee’s access. The support director has authority over whether a proposed account change can enter the business process, while the Chief Security Officer accepts specified residual security risk. The intermediate record links each act to one decision role and one evidence source. A supplier report cannot approve the organization’s connector permissions. A technical test cannot replace the authorized business-risk decision. The conclusion is that responsibility follows the act and authority, not a general label applied to the whole system.

Responsibility control. The decision workflow checks that each consequential act has one responsible role, stated authority, evidence source, escalation route, and review date. It holds approval when any field is missing or conflicting. The measure is complete assignments divided by all consequential acts in the system map. Maintaining the map and resolving conflicts take staff time. Informal decisions outside the workflow can bypass it. Recovery withdraws the unsupported decision and escalates it to a role with authority. A complete assignment shows who must decide, not that the decision is correct.

21.2 Identify applicable obligations

The same technical system can face different duties when its use, location, operator, or date changes. A jurisdiction is a legal territory whose rules may apply. Legal scope is the set of facts that brings a person, organization, system, or act under a rule. An effective date is the date from which a provision applies.

The consolidated EU AI Act text applies generally from August 2, 2026, while amended Article 113 sets later dates for specified Annex III and Annex I systems.3 The dates differ across provisions, so one headline date cannot replace provision-level checking. In the United States health setting, the Department of Health and Human Services stated in its August 7, 2026 review that the current Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act Security Rule remained in effect while proposed cybersecurity changes had not become a final rule.4

These examples show the process rather than a global legal survey. A final decision needs current primary law and qualified interpretation for the organization’s actual locations and sector. Once binding duties and contract terms are identified, voluntary frameworks can organize the remaining work without being mistaken for law.

Scope trace. The organization records five inputs before applying a rule. The inputs cover the operating and user locations. They also cover the affected person and intended use, together with the decision date. A hypothetical credit-scoring use and an internal alert-triage use share a model supplier but have different intended purposes. The legal reviewer checks the current provision and effective date for each use. The intermediate record marks the credit use for provision-level analysis and leaves the alert-triage use outside that specific classification pending other duties. The observed result is a scoped question. It does not classify the model in general.

Dated scope gate. The legal reviewer checks the five inputs against current primary text and records the provision, date, reviewer, and unresolved facts. The reviewer either allows or postpones the proposed use, and an allowed use can carry limits. Coverage is reviewed obligations divided by all candidate obligations identified for the use. Qualified review, updates, and supplier fact gathering create ongoing cost. A stale summary, changed use, or missing location can invalidate the result. Because the record does not prove compliance beyond its facts, law, and date, recovery pauses the affected function and preserves the prior permitted path. The legal review then repeats with current facts.

21.3 Choose useful frameworks

A framework organizes outcomes or practices used to structure risk work without specifying one way to carry them out. CSF 2.0 presents a technology-neutral hierarchy of cybersecurity outcomes and states that it does not prescribe how organizations achieve them.5 It can connect risk work across teams, but it is not an AI control catalogue and does not demonstrate control quality.

A management system is a connected set of policies, roles, processes, and reviews used to meet organizational objectives. The public ISO page describes ISO/IEC 42001 as requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an AI management system.6 Clause-level claims require the licensed normative text, so the public page supports only this scope description.

An organization may use a crosswalk, a scoped mapping between related items in different sources. It records the relationship and the gap instead of treating differently scoped documents as interchangeable. Those categories now guide the data, documentation, supplier, and contract questions in the remaining sections.

Crosswalk comparison. The organization starts with the concrete decision “Who can expand the assistant to customer data?” CSF 2.0 supplies outcomes for responsibility, supplier risk, and access control. The AI Risk Management Framework supplies AI context, measurement, and risk-management questions. ISO/IEC 42001 supplies management-system requirements only to the extent available in the licensed text. The intermediate crosswalk records the relevant item, the system evidence, and any uncovered question. The observed gap is the actual legal basis for customer-data use. No framework row fills that gap. The conclusion is that a crosswalk organizes evidence but does not turn guidance into law or prove compliance.

Crosswalk control. The review register checks every selected framework or standard item against a concrete system record and marks missing evidence or scope. It prevents a framework row from closing a legal or technical question that the source does not answer. Coverage is supported mappings divided by all mappings used in the decision. Building and maintaining the register costs review time. Broad labels and copied mappings can hide a gap. Recovery reopens the affected decision and obtains the missing source or test. A complete crosswalk shows organized coverage, not control effectiveness or legal compliance.

21.4 Data and intellectual property rights

Data can be technically accessible while still being unavailable for collection, training, retrieval, or reuse. Collection permission is the legal, contractual, or organizational basis for obtaining data. A reuse right permits use of material for a later purpose. Confidential information is information protected from unauthorized disclosure by duty or agreement.

NIST recommends categorizing generative AI content by third-party rights and setting policies for how third-party intellectual property and training data are used, stored, and protected.7 This guidance identifies governance work. It does not decide ownership, infringement, fair use, database rights, trade secrets, privacy duties, or license enforcement.

A hypothetical organization may have permission to store a customer document for service delivery but no permission to use it for model training. Retrieval for an authorized employee may also differ from disclosure to an external model supplier. Current jurisdiction-specific primary law and qualified interpretation remain necessary for substantive legal conclusions. The permissions and restrictions determine which decision and change records need to remain available.

Permission control. The data gateway checks the data class, person or service identity, intended purpose, destination, retention rule, and recorded permission before collection, retrieval, training, or external transfer. It allows, removes fields, or blocks the operation and logs the basis. Tests report allowed and denied flows over all purpose-destination cases. Classification and legal review add cost. Copied data, wrong labels, or supplier reuse outside the observed interface can defeat the control. Recovery revokes access and removes affected copies where possible. Rights questions receive qualified review. A technical denial does not settle the underlying legal interpretation.

21.5 Record and review decisions

A responsible role cannot review a system if the reason for a decision disappeared with a meeting. A decision record captures the options, evidence, responsible role, date, conditions, and unresolved issues behind a decision. Related records describe the system, tests, oversight authority, access, incidents, and changes.

The AI Risk Management Framework connects system context, intended purpose, task, legal risk, human oversight, testing, and lifecycle decisions across its MAP, MEASURE, and MANAGE functions.8 For covered high-risk systems, the EU AI Act connects technical documentation, automated logging, information for deployers, human oversight, and deployer monitoring duties.9 These sources do not establish one record format or universal retention period.

The documentation set follows the system boundary. It links versions and decisions without claiming that the record itself proves the system is safe. A human oversight assignment identifies the person or role with review and intervention authority. A change record preserves evidence of a system change and its assessment. These records supply the questions and evidence requests used in supplier assurance.

Decision-record test. A model version changes on 4 October in this hypothetical case. The prior record approved version V7 for policy summaries with read-only retrieval. The change record identifies version V8, the supplier notice, affected tests, responsible reviewer, and the decision to hold expansion. The team can trace each test result back to V7 or V8. If the record contained only “model updated,” a later reviewer could not tell which evidence remained relevant. The observed result tests whether the documentation supports a decision rather than whether a form was completed.

Change control. The release workflow checks the system version, affected assumptions, required tests, oversight assignment, and approval before routing users to the change. It either allows release or keeps the accepted version. A release can carry restrictions. The measure is fully linked material changes divided by all detected material changes. Version tracking and review delay delivery. An emergency change or silent supplier update can bypass the workflow, and complete documentation still cannot show that every failure was detected. Recovery restores the accepted configuration and reconstructs missing records from logs and supplier notices.

21.6 Verify supplier claims

A certificate or audit title can appear relevant while covering the wrong service, location, control period, or customer configuration. Assurance scope identifies the systems, services, locations, controls, and period covered by evidence. A subprocessor is another party used by a supplier to process data or deliver service. The review also records exclusions, shared responsibilities, and customer actions required for the assurance to apply.

CSF 2.0 calls for coordination of supplier roles and inclusion of security requirements in contracts. It also calls for due diligence before a relationship and risk monitoring during it.10 NIST also recommends contract clauses that permit evaluation of third-party generative AI processes and standards.11 A service-specific review still needs to establish the audit quality and the relevance of supplier controls to the organization’s system.

The evidence packet lacks a strong public source on detailed scope and customer-control sections in common assurance reports. The gap remains. The review compares the assurance scope with the actual data flow and service configuration, then lists every uncovered dependency. Those gaps become contract and exit requirements.

Supplier-scope comparison. The hypothetical supplier report covers inference service S1 in region R1 for January through June. The organization uses S1 in region R2 from August and supplies its own connector. The reviewer records the service, region, period, supplier control, and customer control in the comparison. The observed gaps are region R2, the later operating period, and the customer connector. The report may still provide useful evidence for covered controls, but it cannot support a conclusion about the uncovered configuration. The responsible role requests current evidence and records the connector as an organization duty.

Assurance control. The procurement gate compares service, region, period, configuration, subprocessors, supplier controls, and customer duties with the actual deployment. It either accepts the evidence or leaves the related use unapproved. Acceptance can carry a condition. Coverage is matched scope items divided by all scope items required for the decision. Obtaining and reviewing evidence adds supplier and internal cost. A stale report or omitted customer control can produce a false assurance. Recovery restricts the service and disables the uncovered path. Work can move to the approved fallback. A matched report supports only its stated scope and does not prove the whole application secure.

21.7 Plan contractual exit

Dependence becomes difficult to change after data, connectors, workflows, and staff practices accumulate around one service. Portability is the ability to move data, configurations, and necessary artifacts to another service. Termination assistance is supplier support for an orderly end or transfer. A continuity term is a contract condition intended to preserve essential operations during disruption or exit.

NIST recommends reviewing AI supplier contracts for termination, responsibility, incident notice, response times, support availability, and service levels.12 CSF 2.0 includes supplier participation in incident activity and risk management after a service agreement ends.13 These are governance outcomes. Contract language and enforceability require separate legal work.

A hypothetical exit test exports allowed records and removes connector access. It then revokes service credentials and checks deletion evidence. The fallback workflow runs after those checks. Jurisdiction-specific clauses and evidence about practical exit testing remain open research needs. The resulting responsibility, supplier, and continuity record provides the input for security investment decisions in Chapter 22.

Exit control. The exit workflow checks required data, configurations, logs, open cases, credentials, supplier deletion evidence, and fallback readiness. It completes termination only after each item is transferred, retained under an approved basis, or recorded as unresolved. Measures include recovered required items over all required items and revoked credentials over all active credentials. Portability tests, standby service, and supplier support add ongoing cost. Proprietary formats, supplier delay, or a shared dependency can block exit. Recovery keeps the manual path active and limits new data while unresolved items are escalated. A supplier deletion statement does not by itself prove removal from every copy.

Note

Chapter checkpoint. A supplier operates inference, the organization selects customer data, and an internal support director approves account changes. Assign responsibility for model service operation, retrieval eligibility, and the final account decision. State one evidence item and one limit for each assignment.

Answer. The supplier is responsible for the contracted operation of the model service, supported by service records and limited to the contract and assurance scope. The organization role that controls retrieval is responsible for eligible customer fields, supported by permission and retrieval tests and limited by current law and data rights. The support director is responsible for the account decision under the organization’s authority, supported by approval and action logs and limited by the director’s approval range. Each assignment remains limited to its stated act and authority.
