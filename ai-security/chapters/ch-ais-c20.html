20  Managing organizational AI use

An organization-wide adoption process connects visible AI uses to acceptable-use rules, account and connector controls, and employee support.

Security question. How can an organization allow useful AI work without losing sight of systems, data, permissions, and changes?

Organization setting. Employees use approved assistants, purchased software with AI features, and some unapproved services in examples that do not describe a real organization or product.

One approved assistant is only one entry in an organization’s AI inventory. Each additional service or embedded feature needs its own task, data, connector, action, supplier, and review record.

20.1 Organizational AI inventory

Policy cannot govern an AI use that the organization cannot see. An enterprise AI inventory is a controlled record of AI systems and AI-enabled services used by the organization. An embedded AI feature is an AI capability included inside another purchased product. A data flow records movement of data among users, systems, suppliers, and stores.

The NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0 separates inventories of software, services, supplier services, data, metadata, and authorized internal and external data flows.1 The AI Risk Management Framework also includes maintaining an inventory of AI systems according to organizational risk priorities.2 Completeness and currentness require separate discovery evidence.

For each entry, the record links the business task, responsible owner, users, model or service, data types, connectors, data flows, action permissions, supplier, and review date. Discovery may combine procurement and identity records. Expense data, network observations, and employee reports add other views. These are common inventory practices, and their coverage depends on the available evidence. The resulting population defines which uses the acceptable-use rules govern.

Inventory trace. The hypothetical organization compares four discovery inputs for one month: procurement lists an approved assistant and identity logs show two AI services. Expense records show a third service, while an employee report identifies an AI feature inside a support product. The intermediate state is a reconciliation table with service identity, business task, data class, connector, responsible role, and review date. One expense entry has no responsible role and one embedded feature has no data-flow record. The observed result is four candidate uses, two complete records, and two unresolved records. The conclusion treats the inventory as evidence-linked records with visible gaps. It does not claim completeness.

Inventory control. The service that maintains the inventory reconciles procurement, identity, expense, network, and employee reports and marks incomplete entries for review. It allows onboarding only for a record with the required task, data, connector, responsible role, and review date. Coverage is confirmed records divided by all candidate uses found through the stated inputs. Discovery and reconciliation require staff time and can miss personal accounts or hidden product features. Because high coverage across these inputs does not prove that every AI use has been found, recovery suspends unresolved connectors or routes the task to an approved service.

20.2 Acceptable use rules

A general instruction to “use AI responsibly” does not tell an employee if a real task is allowed. An acceptable-use rule sets conditions on permitted AI tasks, data, outputs, or actions. A consequential output can materially affect a person, asset, obligation, or decision. A safe alternative is an approved method offered when the requested AI use is restricted.

NIST recommends updating generative AI acceptable-use policies to cover proprietary and open-source technology and data, as well as contractors and other third parties.3 This is governance guidance, not a complete policy or evidence that publication alone changes behavior.

In a hypothetical organization, an employee may summarize public material in an approved assistant but may not paste customer secrets into an unapproved service. A generated contract clause requires legal review, and a generated payment request cannot execute a transaction. When a rule blocks a useful task, the safe alternative might be a restricted internal assistant or a human review channel. The rules become enforceable only when account and connector permissions match them.

Use control. The application and connector check the employee identity, task, data class, target service, and requested action. They either allow the request or block it. A field filter can change the request before the application shows the safe alternative. Tests report blocked prohibited cases and blocked allowed cases over all policy cases. Classification, support, and exception handling add cost and delay. Copying data through an unmanaged device or misclassifying a field can bypass the rule. Recovery revokes exposed access and removes retained data where the service permits. Work then moves to the approved path. A passed policy test does not prove employee behavior outside managed channels.

20.3 Limit accounts and connectors

An approved service can still exceed policy if a broad connector or shared account bypasses individual access limits. A connector is an integration that lets an AI application read from or act on another system. The security decision identifies the human or service identity used for each call. Its permission scope states the resources and operations allowed to that identity.

CSF 2.0 calls for identities and credentials for users, services, and hardware to be managed. It also calls for permissions and authorizations to be defined, enforced, reviewed, and limited through least privilege and separation of duties.4 These outcomes do not specify an identity product or prove that a connector applies the intended rule.

This identity chain applies from employee onboarding through offboarding. The application authenticates the employee, the connector uses a distinct service identity or delegated token, the target service performs a current authorization check, and logs connect the request to both identities. Shared credentials break this chain. Review dates and revocation tests show whether access changes take effect. These details provide concrete evidence for procurement and onboarding.

Revocation test. In this hypothetical test, employee E17 loses access to customer case C44 at 10:00. At 10:05 the employee submits a request for a C44 summary. The application stores an earlier conversation, but the connector sends E17’s current identity to the case system. The case system denies access, so the application records the denial without receiving the customer record. The observed result meets the expected result. If the connector had used a shared service account and returned the record, the test would show that approval of the AI service did not enforce the employee’s current permissions.

Control review. The target case system enforces access from the current employee identity and record identifier. The connector passes through the denial and clears any queued result for that record. A revocation test reports denied former permissions over all revoked user-resource pairs, plus time from source revocation to effective denial. Delegated identity and frequent checks add integration and latency cost. Cached content, shared credentials, or a second connector can bypass the path. Because one successful pair does not establish complete offboarding, recovery purges affected caches and revokes connector tokens before the full revoked set is retested.

20.4 Approve specific use cases

A supplier review has little value when it examines a brand while leaving the intended use, data, and actions unspecified. A pilot limit is a temporary restriction on users, data, actions, duration, or scale during evaluation. Supplier due diligence examines the supplier before a relationship begins. A time-limited exception is an approved deviation that expires or requires renewed review.

NIST recommends procurement checks for intellectual property, privacy, security, embedded AI, ongoing monitoring, model and tool changes, and incident or vulnerability records.5 CSF 2.0 also calls for due diligence before a supplier relationship and continued recording, treatment, and monitoring of supplier risk.6 These lists do not certify a supplier or settle sector-specific contract duties.

The onboarding record ties the use case to permitted data, action limits, identity design, supplier evidence, acceptance tests, pilot expiry, and an accountable decision. A time-limited exception records why an ordinary requirement cannot yet be met and when renewed review occurs. The people operating the approved use then need task-specific competence and a practical route for help.

Approval control. A procurement reviewer checks the use record, supplier scope, required contract terms, pilot limits, test results, exception expiry, and decision authority. The reviewer approves, restricts, or postpones onboarding. The review reports complete approvals over all proposed uses and overdue exceptions over all active exceptions. Supplier review and negotiation consume legal and engineering time. A product update or unreported subprocessor can invalidate the evidence after approval. Recovery disables the feature or connector and returns users to the stated alternative while review is renewed. Approval does not certify the supplier beyond the inspected service and period.

20.5 Employee skills and support

Employees encounter the system at the point where an unreliable answer, suspicious instruction, or unexpected request becomes a business decision. Human oversight assigns review or intervention to a person with sufficient authority and support. A help channel is a specified route for advice, reporting, or escalation. Training needs to match the assigned task and the decisions the employee can make.

CSF 2.0 distinguishes general awareness from training for specialized roles, with both tied to the knowledge and skills needed to perform work while considering cybersecurity risk.7 Within its scope, the European Union AI Act requires deployers of covered high-risk systems to assign human oversight to people with suitable competence, training, authority, and support.8 That legal rule is limited to the systems and dates covered by the Act.

A hypothetical customer-support employee may need to verify a refund instruction through the case system, while an administrator needs to inspect connector permissions and logs. Questions and incident reports pass through the help channel, so employees do not have to guess. Reports, repeated confusion, and support demand become evidence for adoption and change review.

Competence check. The employee receives a synthetic message that asks for a refund and includes an assistant-generated bank-account change. The task is to decide what can proceed. The expected path ignores the generated payment instruction and verifies the case in the authoritative support system. The account change then goes to the established review channel. The observed exercise result records the employee’s choice, the evidence consulted, and whether escalation occurred. A correct answer supports competence for this scenario only. It does not show readiness for administration, incident response, or legal review.

Oversight control. The business workflow requires the authoritative record and the specified approval role before a consequential change can proceed. The competence test reports correct decisions over all assigned scenarios and records unsafe approvals and unnecessary escalations separately. Training, review, and support time are operating costs. Time pressure, ambiguous cases, or an unavailable help channel can defeat the human step. Recovery stops the transaction and uses the ordinary manual process. Staff review any action already taken. A high exercise score does not prove reliable performance during real incidents.

20.6 Review adoption and change

High use does not by itself show that adoption is safe or valuable. An adoption measure is a count or rate describing approved use of an AI capability. Policy friction is delay or work created by a policy during ordinary tasks. A retirement trigger is an observed condition that starts removal or replacement review.

CSF 2.0 calls for policy review when requirements, threats, technology, or mission change. It also calls for changes and exceptions to be assessed, recorded, and tracked.9 The framework does not define AI adoption measures or universal retirement thresholds. The evidence packet also lacks validated examples of measures that avoid rewarding unsafe volume or discouraging incident reporting.

The review keeps that limit visible. A rise in reports can reflect growing abuse or better awareness. Both causes may be present. Low use can reflect poor value or excessive friction, while incomplete discovery offers another explanation. New model capabilities, contract terms, connectors, or data access can invalidate the original approval even when usage is stable. The resulting practice, exceptions, and open questions become inputs to the responsibility analysis in Chapter 21.

Adoption review record. The example below uses hypothetical values for one quarterly review.

The reviewer compares observed use with the approved task, data, accounts, connectors, and support route. The conclusion is to continue the policy summary pilot with a block on customer fields and focused employee support. The proposed email connector remains outside approval because it changes available data and actions.

Change control. The application configuration checks the approved connector list and data rules before enabling a supplier feature. The review board checks new capabilities, terms, regions, permissions, and incident results before expanding the approval. Measures use changed systems over all inventoried approved systems and policy violations over all inspected requests. Review and field filtering create cost and user friction. Supplier-side activation or incomplete inventory data can bypass the gate. Because stable usage and answer quality do not prove that the boundary remained unchanged, recovery disables the added capability and restores the accepted configuration. Work moves to the documented fallback.

Note

Chapter checkpoint. A supplier update adds an email connector to an approved assistant that summarizes policy. Usage and answer quality remain unchanged. Can the existing approval continue without review?

Answer. No. The connector changes the data flow, possible content, and account permissions even though usage and answer quality did not change. The adoption record should mark the connector as outside the approved boundary and keep it disabled. The record must also identify the responsible role and tests required for a new decision.
