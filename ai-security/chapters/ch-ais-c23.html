23  Adapting controls to the setting

This chapter transfers the reference frame to sector settings by stating which assets, duties, permissions, and consequences change before selecting controls.

Security question. Which assumptions, controls, and evidence change when the users, data, authority, and consequences change?

Organization setting. Each case is hypothetical. The examples isolate design differences and do not establish legal compliance, clinical safety, financial fitness, or product security.

Connection through the book. Chapter 22 supplied a common decision record. This chapter applies it to five settings, then compares what changed. Chapter 24 reopens the assumptions again for edge, federated, physical, persistent-agent, and frontier systems.

Compare every setting with the same fields before reading its details: user population, data, application authority, applicable duty, possible consequence, scale, fallback, and recovery path. When a field changes, review the related control and evidence claim again. Unchanged fields retain their earlier records.

23.1 Enterprise knowledge and customer support

An enterprise assistant may answer a low-impact policy question or expose another customer’s records through the same text interface. The analysis starts with the tenant boundary, retrieval boundary, user identity, eligible records, and any account action that the application can request. A disputed answer is an answer whose correctness or authority is challenged and resolved outside the model.

The NIST Privacy Framework calls for inventories of systems, roles, people, data actions, purposes, data elements, processing environments, and mapped interactions.1 The 2026 OWASP list separates prompt injection, sensitive information disclosure, excessive agency, misinformation, and vector or embedding weaknesses for analysis.2 These frameworks organize the review. They do not show that a weakness exists in the hypothetical assistant or that a proposed control works.

The request path authenticates the employee and filters eligible records before retrieval. The application labels the source and generates an answer. It sends any proposed account action to a separate authorization service. A disputed answer returns to an authoritative record or responsible specialist. This relatively low physical-harm case supplies a comparison point for health information, where an answer can influence care.

Control review. The system of record checks the employee identity and tenant eligibility before returning a document, while the action service checks any account operation separately. Tests report denied cross-tenant requests over all unauthorized pairs and corrected disputed answers over all reviewed answers. Per-request checks, source links, and specialist review add latency and staff cost. Cached records or an alternate connector can bypass the intended route, and these tests cannot establish answer correctness for unreviewed topics. Recovery removes exposed context and revokes affected access. It restores the accepted connector and routes disputed work to the ordinary support process.

23.2 Health information assistants

A health assistant changes the stakes when it reads regulated records or appears to direct care. Electronic protected health information means electronic health information within the legal scope of the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). Clinical authority is permission and responsibility to make or direct a clinical decision. Role-appropriate access limits access to what a person’s assigned work requires.

Within its scope, the HIPAA Security Rule requires administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for electronic protected health information, including access suited to assigned roles.3 The Department of Health and Human Services also requires periodic evaluation after environmental changes such as new technology or newly recognized risks.4 These duties address information security compliance. They do not establish clinical safety or model accuracy.

The hypothetical assistant may summarize an authorized patient’s record for a clinician but cannot diagnose, prescribe, or change an order. Identity, record scope, output labeling, clinical review, correction, and emergency fallback are separate control points. Clinical safety, medical-device scope, and evidence for human review still require use-specific regulation and clinical evaluation. Financial authority provides the next comparison because the system may initiate a reversible or irreversible monetary action.

Control review. The health-record system checks clinician identity, patient relationship, and requested fields. The clinical workflow treats the output as a draft and permits only an authorized clinician to enter an order. Tests report wrong-patient disclosures over all unauthorized patient pairs and unsupported statements over all reviewed summaries. Access checks and clinical review consume time and can delay care. Copied text, stale role data, or use outside the workflow can bypass the controls. Recovery removes the draft and corrects the record. The established clinical or emergency path remains active while staff investigate any affected decision. A passing information-security test does not establish clinical safety or legal compliance.

23.3 Financial workflows

A financial assistant may classify an alert. Other configurations may prepare or approve a transfer. Those acts need different authority. Transaction authority is permission to initiate, approve, modify, or reverse a financial action. An approval limit sets the maximum value or action class an identity may approve. A reversal is a controlled action that restores an earlier financial state where the rules permit it.

Under the EU AI Act, specified systems used to evaluate a person’s creditworthiness or establish a credit score appear in Annex III, while systems used to detect financial fraud are excluded from that item.5 Classification still depends on intended use, conditions, exceptions, and applicable dates. CSF 2.0 calls for identities to be authenticated and for permissions and authorizations to be defined, enforced, reviewed, and separated by duty.6 Those general outcomes do not define payment rules or reversal rights.

In the hypothetical workflow, the model can explain an alert and prepare a proposed action. The payment system verifies the employee identity, amount, approval limit, separation of duties, and current account state before execution. Logs connect proposal, approval, execution, and reversal. A final case requires current banking, payment, securities, credit, and recordkeeping law for one jurisdiction. The public-service case adds legal effects, explanation, and challenge rights.

Financial action trace. The operating mode is inference with a proposed action, not autonomous execution. Employee E8 asks the assistant to prepare a 14,000 currency-unit transfer after reviewing a fraud alert. The assistant returns the proposed recipient, amount, and reason. The payment system then checks E8’s current identity and 10,000 approval limit. The intermediate decision is a denial followed by escalation to a second authorized employee. The observed result is no transfer and a linked proposal, denial, and escalation record. All values are hypothetical. The trace shows that fluent financial text does not replace transaction authority.

Control review. The payment system checks current identity, amount, approval limit, separated approval, recipient state, and transaction status. It allows, denies, or holds the transfer and records each decision. Tests report unauthorized transfers over all attempted restricted transfers and mistaken denials over all allowed cases. Independent approval and reversal capacity add cost and delay. Shared credentials, a second payment route, or a stale limit can bypass the path. Recovery cancels pending work and reverses eligible transactions. It freezes affected credentials and escalates irreversible loss. A zero-failure test does not establish performance against untested fraud or operational error.

23.4 Public-sector services

A public service may affect access to benefits or a person’s legal status. It can also influence emergency response or access to justice. Contestability is the ability to challenge a decision and seek correction or review. A legal effect changes a person’s legal rights, duties, or status. Accessibility is the ability of people with differing needs to use the service.

EU AI Act Annex III includes specified uses involving essential public benefits, emergency response, law enforcement, migration, justice, and democratic processes.7 Article 86 provides a limited explanation right when a covered Annex III high-risk system contributes to a decision with legal effects or comparably serious adverse effects.8 Both claims have express conditions, exceptions, and dates. They do not create a general explanation right for every public-sector output.

The hypothetical service keeps the AI output as reviewable input to an authorized decision process. The record identifies the legal basis, evidence, human authority, explanation route, correction path, accessibility needs, and appeal mechanism. This setting emphasizes bounded public authority. The consumer-platform case changes the scale and permits open user access, which shifts attention toward abuse volume and resource limits.

Control review. The case-management system checks the legal basis, required evidence, authorized official, notice, and challenge status before a consequential decision takes effect. It approves, returns, or blocks the decision and preserves the record used. Tests report complete notices and successful challenge routing over all consequential cases in scope, plus decisions corrected after review. Accessibility and human review add time and service cost. Informal channels, incomplete records, or inaccessible notices can defeat the process. Because process evidence does not prove the underlying decision was lawful or correct, recovery pauses the effect where permitted and corrects the record. The person then enters the established review or appeal path.

23.5 Consumer platforms

An open consumer platform receives high request volume from users whose identity, intent, and content are uncertain. Account abuse is unauthorized or deceptive use of a user account. Impersonation and automated sign-up can occur alongside harmful content. Tool misuse or resource exhaustion may follow even when each request appears minor.

OWASP describes its August 2026 Large Language Model (LLM) Top 10 as a community guide to critical risks in LLM-powered applications.9 Its 2026 list treats excessive agency and unbounded consumption as distinct application risks.10 The list supplies a taxonomy. Prevalence and mitigation effectiveness require separate evidence.

The application can bind rate limits to accounts, devices, payment state, and action classes. It can require stronger verification before consequential actions and give users a reporting path. A hypothetical limit of one hundred requests per hour remains only a design value until load and abuse tests show its effects on legitimate work, attack cost, and service availability. The cross-setting comparison includes this unresolved evidence.

Rate-limit comparison. A hypothetical test replays the same 1,000 ordinary requests and 500 automated abuse requests under limits of 50, 100, and 200 requests per account per hour. The intermediate record separates blocked abuse, blocked ordinary work, queue time, and account switching. At 50, the system blocks more abuse but also blocks a simulated support surge. At 200, it permits more abuse. The test does not choose a universal value because device identity, distributed accounts, and real demand remain uncertain. Its conclusion is that the selected limit needs both abuse and legitimate-work evidence under the platform’s operating conditions.

Control review. The gateway checks account, device, payment state, request rate, and action class before admitting work. It allows, slows, challenges, or blocks the request. The test reports blocked abuse over all simulated abuse and blocked ordinary work over all ordinary requests, together with queue time and infrastructure cost. Stronger checks increase user friction and support demand. Distributed accounts or shared infrastructure can bypass or overwhelm the limit, while the replay cannot estimate real abuse prevalence or prove resilience at a larger scale. Recovery reduces expensive features and protects essential paths. Legitimate users receive a support route.

23.6 Comparison across cases

Each case uses the same system-mapping method, but its assets, authority, duties, error costs, and recovery evidence differ. A transfer analysis compares what changes when a design enters another setting. Oversight depth combines the frequency, authority, and technical detail of human review.

The AI Risk Management Framework states that risk management is tailored to context and that priorities account for organizational goals, affected people, legal requirements, and available resources.11 CSF 2.0 also states that implementation varies with risk, tolerance, mission, technology, and requirements.12 These sources support tailoring. They do not rank the hypothetical cases or make their controls equivalent.

The method remains constant while each case changes the facts. Enterprise support emphasizes tenant and retrieval separation. Health adds regulated records and clinical boundaries. Finance adds transaction authority and reversal. Public services add legal effects and contestability. Consumer platforms add open access and scale. This analysis shows which assumptions need renewed examination when Chapter 24 introduces exposed devices, distributed training, physical action, persistent agents, and frontier capabilities.

Sector comparison. Each row below changes one condition in the recurring hypothetical organization. It does not establish legal compliance or rank sectors.

The comparison uses the same mapping method while changing the user population, data, authority, consequence, and expected evidence. A control transfers only when those conditions and its mechanism remain the same.

Transfer control. The architecture review compares the source and target setting for data, identity, authority, duty, consequence, operating scale, fallback, and recovery. It accepts a control unchanged only when its enforcing component and checked state remain valid. The measure is retested transferred controls over all controls reused in the target setting. Reassessment adds delivery and specialist cost. Superficial similarity can hide a changed legal or physical condition. Because successful transfer of one control does not validate the rest of the design, recovery withdraws the transferred approval and returns the task to the prior setting or manual route.

Note

Chapter checkpoint. Move the enterprise support assistant into the health-information setting. Which parts of the earlier design can remain, and which decision must be reopened first?

Answer. Identity checks, source links, retrieval filtering, and a disputed-answer route can remain as candidate controls. Their tests must use health-record roles and data. Authority must be reviewed first. An employee-support answer cannot become clinical direction merely because the same model and retrieval pattern are used. The organization needs current legal scope, clinical evaluation, authorized oversight, correction, and emergency fallback before approving the changed use.
